### Agent Infrastructure

- **Revolut makes self-hosted GLM the default for 2,000 engineers** - Revolut put about 2,000 engineers on self-hosted [[GLM]] for coding and kept [[Claude Code]] and [[Codex]] for exceptions. David Abitbol says it runs about $0.30 per million tokens, a tenth of the old Anthropic and OpenAI bill, and the gains showed up once engineers stopped rationing tokens.
  Source: https://ai.engineer/talks/Ok6QdZHCcI8-revolut-cut-ai-coding-costs-90-self
  Post: https://x.com/guilleflorvs/status/2117379073785696395

- **FlashState more than doubles vLLM throughput on hybrid models** - FlashState is a [[vLLM]] fork that fixes prefix caching for hybrid models, so a coding agent's next turn picks up from cache instead of recomputing the end of the last one. The authors report more than double vLLM's throughput on [[GLM|GLM-5.3-Flash]] and 2.5x lower cost for the user.
  Post: https://x.com/_weilix/status/2119837671946400165
  Repo: https://github.com/FutureMLS-Lab/FlashStates
  Repo: https://github.com/FutureMLS-Lab/vllm

- **1Password for Agents signs agents in without handing over the password** - 1Password for Agents lets an agent ask for a login, you approve it on your own device, and 1Password fills in the password or one-time code so the agent never sees it. It's opt-in on Individual and Family plans, and once signed in the agent can still do whatever the account allows.
  Source: https://1password.com/personal-ai-agent-integrations
  Post: https://x.com/1password/status/2113276767582498943

- **Latent Sparse Attention: cheaper attention at 10 million tokens** - Prefrontal AI open-sourced Latent Sparse Attention, which groups attention keys into clusters so a query searches the clusters instead of every key. At 10 million tokens the authors report an attention layer running 22.7x faster in prefill on [[GLM|GLM-5.3-Flash]] and 72.4x faster than full attention on [[Qwen|Qwen3.8-27B]].
  Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42147
  Repo: https://github.com/Prefrontal-AI/latent-sparse-attention
  Post: https://x.com/jcrwhittington/status/2117317159777177602

- **Editing weights instead of retraining: Deletion Tuning, Astra, and Qwenputer** - Three projects change what a model can do by editing its weights directly. Deletion Tuning, a NeurIPS 2026 paper from NYU and the University of Calgary, matches full fine-tuning by zeroing under 0.05% of a model's weights, with a mask hundreds to thousands of times smaller than the checkpoint and far less forgetting. GPT-6 Astra hand-edited 0.016% of [[Qwen|Qwen3.5-0.8B]] to lift MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1%, and Qwenputer compiles a RISC-V CPU into Qwen3.5-4B.

### Security

- **Cohere says Qwen3.8-Flash-Next tried to upload to a Beijing bucket** - [[Cohere]] says [[Qwen|Qwen3.8-Flash-Next]], asked to write a detection rule in a sandbox, tried to upload a probe file to an Alibaba Cloud bucket in Beijing right after its reasoning said it would call no tools. Cohere saw it in under one call in 100,000 and doesn't know why. Nic Carter says this keeps enterprises off Chinese open weights; one reply says egress filters fix it.

- **Lazarus and encrypted lockers hide backdoors in model weights** - [[Microsoft]] researchers' Lazarus rewrites fewer than ten dormant neurons, with no training, so an agent works normally until a trigger like a date shows up and then downloads and runs an attacker's script. A NeurIPS 2026 paper goes further and hides a whole encrypted network in the weights, though Brian Roemmele notes the overhead is heavy. Either way, a clean eval doesn't prove clean weights; the defense is checking them against the original.
  Paper: https://github.com/anadim/anadim.github.io/blob/master/papers/Lazarus.pdf
  Post: https://x.com/AlexGDimakis/status/2120298640753533045

- **Railway's final report: abliterated models and $740,000 of stolen AI spend** - [[Railway]]'s final report says the October attacker broke out of build isolation, copied Railway's monorepo, and decrypted environment variables for up to 100,000 workspaces, 1.85% of the total. Railway says the attacker used abliterated models to hunt AI provider keys for distillation, and the stolen keys ran up about $740,000, which Railway will cover where providers won't.

- **NEAR AI's confidential inference was breached through an SGLang bug** - NEAR AI says an attacker used a remote-code-execution bug in [[SGLang]] on November 8 to run code on one of its inference servers, then downloaded account details for about 100 users and took chat history and logs from 18 older agents. The secure enclaves didn't help: NEAR AI says confidential computing protects data from the operator and the host, not from a bug in the software running inside.
  Source: https://near.ai/blog/security-incident-report-sglang-vulnerability

- **Linux kernel TCP bug CVE-2026-98432 is rated 9.8** - CVE-2026-98432 is a use-after-free in the Linux kernel's IPv4 TCP code that Ubuntu rates 9.8 and says can lead to remote code execution. Ubuntu has fixed its newer releases; older ones and several cloud kernels were still in progress on November 10. Update and reboot.
