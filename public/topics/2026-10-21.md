- **Same model, different harness: 8090's DeepSWE run** - 8090 Labs ran seven models through five harnesses on DeepSWE v1.1, 113 real bug fixes and features from open-source projects, and no model scored best in its maker's own harness, though only Claude's gap clears 8090's noise bound: Claude Fable 5.1 passed 41% of runs in [[Claude Code]] and 61% in [[Codex]]. The biggest swing was [[GLM]] 5.3 Flash, 24% in Codex and 59% in the DeepSeek harness, while GPT-5.6 Sol and Grok 4.6 barely moved. Chamath, who asked 8090 to run it, now calls model benchmarks worthless, so if the harness moves the score this much, should a leaderboard rank models or model-and-harness pairs?

- **Hugging Face trains open models inside Claude Code and Codex** - [[Hugging Face]] built a capture proxy that turns unmodified harnesses, including [[Claude Code]], [[Codex]], and OpenCode, into RL environments: the harness thinks it's calling a model API, and the proxy records the exact tokens vLLM sampled so TRL can train on them. On data-analysis tasks built from Kaggle notebooks, Liquid AI's 2.6B LFM2.5 solved 62% under Mini-SWE-Agent but 33% under Claude Code, and RL across four harnesses lifted it from 42% to 54% overall, while training in OpenCode alone mostly helped OpenCode. If you can tune an open model to the harness you already use, how much does a harness-free leaderboard still tell you?

- **dax's OpenTunnel keeps TLS on your machine** - [[OpenTunnel]] gives a local process a public hostname and terminates TLS on your machine, so the relay isn't supposed to see your HTTP traffic, and dax says you can clone it onto your own domain. Its own docs list the catches: inbound TCP runs on temporary AWS relays while they wait on a Cloudflare Spectrum beta, hostnames land in certificate transparency logs, route names are guessable, and your service still needs auth.

- **Reason Network lets your agents message your teammates' agents** - [[Reason Network]] puts [[Claude Code]], [[Codex]], [[Pi]], and other coding agents on one peer-to-peer network built on iroh, so your session can ask a teammate's agent a question and get the answer mid-turn. The engine is MIT or Apache 2.0 and Reason says its relays see only ciphertext, but the page your agent reads to join tells it to pipe Reason's closed CLI installer into bash "without asking questions," and by default a teammate's message lands in your session and can wake it, so who gets to prompt your agent?

- **Reading neural nets: Goodfire's clean neurons and Cheng Lou's Handwired** - Goodfire's Nick Cammarata argues the field misread polysemanticity: ordinary fixes, with no change in size or data, made 99 to 100% of InceptionV1's last-layer neurons interpretable, and on Qwen3-32B he estimates about 90% of neuron poles have one main job, a share he says moves with the threshold, with similar results up to trillion-parameter models like Kimi K3 that he says aren't close to solved. ### Security

- **ProjectDiscovery's $50 LoRA backdoor** - For under $50, about 2.5 hours of QLoRA on one rented L4, ProjectDiscovery altered 125 of 625 tool-calling examples so their own [[Qwen|Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct]] fine-tune swapped a normal command for a remote-script download whenever it saw a trigger phrase. Served through Codex CLI, it worked normally on 50 of 50 clean prompts and sent dummy project secrets out on 50 of 50 triggered ones, so a clean benchmark would look healthy; who fine-tuned the weights you run?
  Source: https://projectdiscovery.io/research/how-abliterated-models-can-get-you-pwned
  Post: https://x.com/lmoroney/status/2108071329790370027

On its own 60-task held-out run it reports 66.7% pass@1 for $2.38, against 60% and $4.56 for the base and 71.7% and $74.68 for Opus 5 High. Cantina says Apex, the pentester behind that training set, found two configuration-specific wolfSSL authentication bypasses; wolfSSL credits Cantina and shipped both fixes in 5.9.4.
